Can you make 161 Chama pencil out?

Change the assumptions and watch the development funding gap, loan payment, operating cash flow, and debt coverage change. Based on a December 2025 financing scenario for The Cottages at 161 Chama. This is an educational scenario, not a current underwriting statement.

Development assumptions

What happens?

Annual debt service is calculated automatically using a fully amortizing loan with monthly payments. Funding gap = total development cost minus grants and loan. Positive means additional capital is needed; negative means sources exceed uses.

Try a challenge

Can you close the capital gap while keeping debt service coverage at or above 1.20? Increase the grant, reduce costs, or adjust the loan amount, interest rate, term, revenue and expenses. Notice that solving the construction budget does not necessarily solve operating feasibility.

Source: “2 Response to Governor - Cottage Financing 12122025.xlsx,” including Sources & Uses and Schedule C. Original inputs: 20 units; $3,793,840 total development cost; $2,200,000 grant; $1,593,840 loan; $142,650 gross annual rental income; 7% vacancy; $34,000 annual operating expenses including reserves; $80,688 annual loan payment. The original workbook shows $80,688 in annual debt service; this version calculates approximately $80,636 using standard monthly amortization at 3% over 30 years. The small difference reflects payment calculation or rounding conventions. Some spreadsheet subtotals were rounded. No confidential resident information is included.
